# Evidence and model decisions

Rakshak is a reproducible **single-area, 50 Hz simulation** of flexible demand and protected circuits. The experiment asks whether the same contingency can be handled with less critical energy unserved and less restart pickup than a feeder-level benchmark. The policies, seed, eligibility, initial load and clinical backup assumptions are held constant when comparing restart strategies.

The backup is idealized, sized in minutes at the protected load, with no transfer delay, charging, inverter losses or generator start model. It supplies protected circuits only and is tracked separately from grid demand. |

The 60 W bedside monitor and 2 kW clinical ventilation circuit are illustrative planning values. Actual active power depends on equipment and operating condition. |

The simplified feeder comparator omits distribution power flow, voltage and real relay coordination. |
| Curtailment and recovery | [Oyefeso et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.11893v1) motivate compressor protection, heterogeneous loads and coordinated restart. ## Project contribution

Rakshak combines a protected-circuit model with auditable, participation-constrained dispatch for an Indian distribution context. It logs the trigger, relief request and eligible watts. During shortage it prefers to release surplus-absorbing loads, rotates previously curtailed loads behind alternatives, and prefers loads without compressor lockout when otherwise comparable. Restoration orders eligible devices by service target and checks projected frequency and reserve headroom. The comparison exposes the cost through longest device pause, unmet relief and backup energy, alongside frequency and critical energy unserved. Service targets influence the queue; a blocked device can exceed its target. This is a project-level design contribution. Establishing research novelty over all published work requires a separate systematic review.

## Verification boundary

The closed-form damped swing-equation check isolates numerical integration at 50 ms and 25 ms. Regression tests cover sustained absolute load, disturbance signs, appliance eligibility, compressor lockout, fleet aggregation, backup exhaustion, and the separation between grid and backup supply. Scenario comparisons exercise the same entry point as the browser. These checks verify internal behavior under model assumptions. Field validation would require calibrated feeder parameters and measured device availability, active power, response times and cold-load pickup.

## Revised reference profile and validation protocol

The 2026-11-13 revision replaces the earlier quarter-fleet-per-stage baseline. Earlier results above this revision must not be reused. The named reference is the [SRPC task-force report of 27 March 2024](https://www.srpc.kar.nic.in/website/2023/meetings/special/splocc_20240327mom.pdf): 49.4/49.2/49.0/48.8 Hz, 5/6/7/7% relief allocation. Its actual allocation methodology uses peak-demand quantities and regional distributions. Here the reference is the frozen scenario's initial active demand, explicitly a scaled experiment assumption. Each modeled building is a switchable feeder block. Fixed groups are allocated before policy execution, excluding dedicated clinic, cold-chain and water-service buildings. Whole-block rounding can exceed the target by less than one block. A medical home on a mixed block can still lose supply. Report achieved relief rather than assuming commanded relief equals power removed.

All controlled modes include the same AUFLS fallback. The comparator and Rakshak share governor, turbine, reserve, inventory, initial states, fault timing and backup. The unmanaged mode is an experimental control. Reconnection assumes shared operator permission after generation recovery begins and 10 s stable frequency. This is an illustrative operator rule, not an asserted Indian restoration procedure. AUFLS dwell 100 ms, model cutoff 47.5 Hz and DR trigger 49.60 Hz are explicit assumptions. The cutoff terminates the reduced-order frequency trajectory; it is not a calibrated national collapse prediction.

India also uses governor response, ancillary reserves, operator demand management, local fault protection and restoration procedures. A [published Southern Region ADMS example](https://www.srpc.kar.nic.in/website/2022/commercial/m40tcc_42srpc.pdf) depends on overdrawal as well as low frequency and sustained durations. This project does not emulate current nationwide ADMS, df/dt protection, black-start, voltage collapse, transmission constraints or distribution power flow. The example is dated 2022 and must not be mislabeled as a newly verified 2026 setting.

## Service, permission and state

Critical/essential/ordinary/flexible are service-policy classifications. Consent is independent. Critical and essential loads are excluded from discretionary dispatch; ordinary devices also remain uncontrolled unless a reviewed supported configuration changes their role. Only supported flexible loads with consent and building enrollment can participate. Pending requests have no effect. Approval of an appliance does not provision UPS power. Clinical and cold-chain facilities have an ideal protected backup bus; a medical home has no implicit backup.

Minimum AC on-time 60 s, compressor off-time 180 s, maximum pause targets 240 s (AC), 300 s (storage heating), 600 s (EV), restart multipliers and damping parameters are declared illustrative assumptions. If frequency or capacity prevents restoration, the target can be exceeded and is flagged. An exhausted load cannot be selected again during that run. These are service budgets, not validated temperature safety limits. Appliance maintenance/dispatch decisions run at 500 ms; frequency integration and energy accounting run at 50 ms. Grid outage restoration preserves compressor restart lockout in all modes.

The community water example uses a 750 W small pump (MePDCL utility example), 500 L tank, 250 L initial storage, 1 L/s pump flow, 0.25 L/s draw and 150/450 L switching thresholds. Capacities, flow rates and thresholds are design assumptions, not a municipal installation rating. Water-delivery shortfall is tracked separately. Cold-chain electrical continuity does not establish vaccine temperature compliance.

Requested, served and unserved service energy integrate requested running watts and include backup-served load. Startup transient watts contribute to grid load and restart peak, but are excluded from these service-energy metrics. Surplus absorption is also excluded from requested-service energy. Medical device-minutes count electrical unavailability only. No ECG or physiological inference is made.

Candidate contribution: auditable, consent-aware appliance control with protected-service constraints, alongside a common conventional protection baseline. A full systematic novelty review and external validation are still required. A reviewer workflow is an application feature, not a novel frequency-control algorithm.

Run `python -B benchmarks/research_matrix.py --workers 8` for 20 paired seeds × 5 enrollment levels × 3 shortage magnitudes × 2 policies (600 runs). Each run is 270 simulated seconds at 50 ms. CSV contains individual results and JSON contains paired mean differences with descriptive 95% Student-t intervals. These intervals describe variability of seeded model scenarios, not uncertainty in real Indian-grid parameters. Zero enrollment and small contingencies are included to expose no-benefit cases. Use the four-mode benchmark for restoration ablation, and the regression suite for conservation and numerical checks. All runs freeze the approved inventory revision at their start.

Local feeder-fault scenarios assume an ideal stiff upstream grid at 50 Hz. Upstream import follows the surviving demand. This isolates continuity and backup behavior from the area-frequency experiments. The local fault therefore cannot be interpreted as a national frequency disturbance. The backup-exhaustion preset starts with 0.25 minutes of protected-facility backup and an 85 s fault.
